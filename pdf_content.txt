Problem statement:
The cafeteria needs a centralized relational database for managing cafeterias, counters,
customers, employees, menu items, orders, payments, recipes, ingredients, suppliers, offers,
and feedback. The system should keep cafeteria records organized, maintain accurate order
and payment information, track ingredient stock, and help staff access reliable operational
information.
1. Introduction to the Project Domain
A campus cafeteria handles a large number of food orders in a short period. The same menu
item may be sold many times during a day, while ingredient stock changes continuously
through purchases, consumption, waste, and returns.
Managing these activities requires maintaining customer records, cafeteria and counter
information, menu categories, menu items, recipes, inventory items, suppliers, orders,
payments, offers, and feedback. When these records are maintained manually or in separate
spreadsheets, information can become scattered and difficult to update.
The Cafeteria Management System is a proposed relational database solution that brings
these activities together. It uses structured tables and relationships so that orders, payments,
menu information, and inventory records can be maintained consistently.
2. Need for the Project and Problem Statement
Without a centralized cafeteria database, common operational problems include:
 Incorrect or inconsistent billing information
 Ordering menu items that are not available
 Untracked ingredient consumption and stock changes
 Low-stock items being missed during operations
 Incorrect application of discounts or offers
 Missing or inconsistent payment records
 Difficulty producing daily sales and inventory reports
3. Objectives
The objectives of the project are to:
1. Maintain records for customers, cafeterias, counters, and employees.
2. Maintain menu categories and menu items with price, tax, preparation time, and
availability.
3. Create multi-item orders and keep order totals consistent.
4. Connect menu items with required ingredients through recipe records.
5. Track supplier receipts, ingredient consumption, waste, and returns.
6. Prevent order confirmation when required inventory is insufficient.
7. Record payments that match the final order amount.
8. Apply valid offers according to their defined conditions.
9. Provide organized information for sales, inventory, supplier, and customer reports.
10. Keep the project based on structured relational data.

11. Maintain accurate and timely access to cafeteria information.
4. Scope
Included in the proposed system
 Customer and employee information management
 Cafeteria and counter management
 Menu category and menu item management
 Order and order-item records
 Payment records and order totals
 Recipe and ingredient requirements
 Inventory, supplier, stock purchase, consumption, waste, and return records
 Offers and order-level discounts
 Customer feedback associated with completed orders
Users of the system:
• Customers: place orders and provide feedback for completed orders.
• Cafeteria staff: manage counters, orders, payments, menu availability, and inventory
operations according to their responsibilities.
• Cafeteria managers: oversee cafeteria operations, reports, offers, suppliers, and inventory
records.
5. Project Completion Steps:
1. Gather requirements: Identify the information and operations needed for customers,
cafeteria staff, and management. Decide which cafeteria activities belong in the project.
2. Analyze the requirements: Define the system data, business rules, relationships, and
access responsibilities.
3. Design the system: Prepare the ER diagram, relational database structure, process flow,
and table relationships.
4. Choose the technology: Select MySQL, MySQL Workbench, ERDPlus/diagrams.net,
SQL, and supporting documentation tools.
5. Create the database: Build relational tables for cafeterias, counters, customers,
employees, menu items, orders, payments, recipes, inventory, suppliers, offers, and
feedback.
6. Develop the database operations: Implement SQL statements for inserting, updating,
retrieving, and reporting cafeteria information.
7. Verify the system: Check order processing, payment validation, inventory rules, offer
conditions, feedback rules, and record relationships.
8. Prepare documentation: Document the problem, objectives, scope, database design,
tools, and expected outcomes.
9. Present the project: Demonstrate the basic cafeteria workflow and explain the
relational design and project outcomes.
